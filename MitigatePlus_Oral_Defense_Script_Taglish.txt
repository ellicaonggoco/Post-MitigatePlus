"The City of Manila is one of the most densely populated and disaster-vulnerable cities in the Philippines.

Manila has a population density of over 43,000 residents per square kilometer, making it among the most crowded urban areas in the world. The city sits along several bodies of water including Manila Bay, the Pasig River, and several esteros. These create extreme flood risk during typhoons and heavy monsoon rainfall.

On average, the Philippines is struck by 20 tropical cyclones per year. Manila barangays along waterways can submerge within just a few hours of heavy rainfall, leaving families with little time to evacuate.

Under Republic Act No. 10121, the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010, local Barangay DRRMC units are legally mandated to serve as the primary frontline responders in every disaster event. They are responsible for identifying affected families, distributing relief goods, and coordinating with city-level agencies.

However, these barangay-level operations face serious structural challenges that a paper-based system simply cannot handle at scale. That is the gap MitigatePlus was built to address."

SLIDE 9: Project Context - Operational Bottlenecks
Visual Cue: 897 barangays, damp logbooks, repeat claimants vs unserved families.
Speaker: JM
Spoken Script:
"Through our direct field interviews with relief workers and barangay officials across Manila, we identified three critical operational bottlenecks that appear every single time a disaster strikes.

Bottleneck 1: Resident Verification Failure.
Barangay volunteers at distribution tables cannot quickly verify whether the person in front of them is actually a registered resident. Without real-time digital verification, it is nearly impossible to catch imposters during a high-pressure distribution event.

Bottleneck 2: The One Pack Fits All Problem.
Almost all relief distributions use a flat system: every household receives one standardized pack regardless of family size. For a family of 12 people living in Tondo, one bag runs out within 24 hours, creating a nutrition crisis in the middle of an ongoing disaster.

Bottleneck 3: Duplicate Claims and Palakasan.
Without a real-time digital log, repeat claimants walk from one table to another and collect multiple packs. Meanwhile, vulnerable residents: seniors, bedridden PWDs, and families in interior alleys, often receive nothing.

Each feature of MitigatePlus was designed specifically to eliminate one of these three bottlenecks."

Specific Objective 4: Two Core Algorithmic Engines.

Algorithm 1 is our Smart Recovery Priority Index. It calculates a priority score for each household based on structural damage severity, vulnerable household members such as seniors, infants, and PWDs, and the number of days they have been waiting without relief. SLIDE 23 & 24: Security Testing (Pre vs Post Remediation)
Visual Cue: Security Audit Results, OWASP Top 10 checks, resolved vulnerabilities.
Speaker: Datul
Spoken Script:
"Security is one of the most critical requirements of MitigatePlus, because the system handles sensitive personal data of disaster-affected residents: household member names, home addresses, government ID photos, and relief claim records.

We evaluated security under ISO 25010 and validated against the OWASP Top 10, the globally recognized list of the most critical web application security vulnerabilities.

On Slide 23, our Pre-Remediation audit identified several issues: no rate limiting on login endpoints, making them vulnerable to brute-force attacks; JWT tokens lacking a server-side revocation mechanism; and some API endpoints missing input validation middleware.

On Slide 24, Post-Remediation, all patches were implemented:

Password Encryption: All passwords are hashed using Bcrypt with 10 salt rounds before storage. Even if an unauthorized party accessed our database, they cannot read any user's actual password.

Brute-Force Prevention: We deployed express-rate-limit middleware on all authentication endpoints. After 5 failed attempts, the account is temporarily locked and the IP is throttled.

Duplicate Prevention at Database Level: In MongoDB, we enforced a Compound Unique Index on distributionEventId and householdId. This is enforced at the storage engine level, making it mathematically impossible for the same household to have two release records for the same distribution event. No application-level logic can be bypassed because the database itself rejects the duplicate write."

SLIDE 25: Usability Evaluation - Data Collection and Methodology
Visual Cue: 108 survey participants + 2 IT professionals summary.
Speaker: Datul
Spoken Script:
"For our Usability Evaluation, we designed and implemented a structured data collection process that follows ethical and academic standards.

Let me explain our methodology clearly.

Data Collection Procedure:
Our usability survey was conducted online through a Google Forms survey, which was shared with residents, college students, barangay staff, and IT professionals connected to the target communities in Manila. Before answering the survey, all participants were briefed on the purpose of MitigatePlus and voluntarily agreed to participate after being given the opportunity to explore the system.

The survey consisted of 20 Likert-scale items grouped into four usability categories: Ease of Use, Performance and Efficiency, Learnability, and Satisfaction. Each item was rated on a 5-point scale, where 1 means Strongly Disagree and 5 means Strongly Agree. After the Likert items, two open-ended questions asked respondents to describe the system's strengths and suggest improvements. Responses were collected digitally, ensuring convenience and accuracy for all participants.

Data Management:
All responses were automatically stored in a linked, password-protected Google Sheets spreadsheet. To protect participant confidentiality, identifying details were used only for internal record-keeping and were excluded from all aggregated reports. Only the research team had access to the raw dataset. Raw data will be retained until the manuscript's final defense and archived afterward for record-keeping purposes.

Data Interpretation:
Survey responses were coded numerically using the five-point Likert scale. For each item and category, we calculated the mean, which is the arithmetic average of all responses, and the standard deviation, which measures how spread out the responses are from the mean.

A low standard deviation means most participants closely agreed in their ratings, indicating strong consensus. A high standard deviation means there were more varied opinions among respondents.

Our results were then interpreted using a standardized mean score table, which Chester will now present."

QUESTION 5 (Technical - Race Conditions & Simultaneous Scans):
"What happens if two volunteers scan the exact same QR pass at the exact same millisecond at two different gates?"
Lead Responder: Datul / Raf
Spoken Defense:
"This is a race condition scenario, and we handle it at the database engine level, not at the application level.

In MongoDB, we created a Compound Unique Index on distributionEventId and householdId, enforced at the storage engine level.

When two simultaneous release requests arrive at the API at the same millisecond, MongoDB processes them using atomic document locking. The first request successfully writes the release record. The second request is immediately rejected by the database engine itself with an E11000 Duplicate Key Error before any data is written.

Our backend catches this specific error code, prevents the duplicate release, and fires a real-time Socket.io alert to both scanner devices and the Web Admin fraud stream simultaneously. The panel member's family receives exactly one set of relief goods regardless of how many operators processed the same QR pass at the same time."

QUESTION 6 (Technical - Zero-Connectivity Contingency):
"Paano kung mawalan ng internet sa evacuation area? Makakapag-scan pa ba ang mga tanod?"
Lead Responder: Ellica / Datul
Spoken Defense:
"Yes po, and this is a scenario we specifically planned for.

For residents: the QR Relief Pass is cached in the phone's local AsyncStorage immediately upon approval. Even if the phone is in Airplane Mode or a zero-signal zone, the resident can open and present their QR pass instantly.

For field staff: if the network drops during a distribution event, the scanner app switches to offline mode automatically. Scan records are queued in local device storage. The scanner continues processing claims using the last synchronized household data. When the device reconnects to any network, the offline queue automatically synchronizes with the cloud database in the background.

We acknowledge that the MongoDB-level duplicate interception cannot be enforced in true offline mode. The offline queue is designed to reconcile with the server upon reconnection and flag any conflicts for manual administrator review."

QUESTION 7 (Algorithm 1 - Weighting Justification):

Lead Responder: Chester / JM

"The 40-40-20 weight distribution was not arbitrary. It was derived from DSWD Disaster Assistance Family Access Card guidelines and validated with Sir Ceasar Salvacion of Manila DRRMD.

QUESTION 8 (Algorithm 2 - Why Not Just One Pack Per Family):
"Bakit kailangan pa ng Algorithm 2? Pwede namang tig-iisang pack na lang bawat pamilya?"
Lead Responder: Raf / Chester
Spoken Defense:
"Because household sizes in Manila vary drastically, and a flat one-pack policy causes real nutritional harm to large families.

A family of 2 and a family of 12 do not have the same food requirement. Giving both the same single pack is inequitable: the 12-person family runs out within 24 hours and is forced to re-enter distribution lines, creating chaos and potential repeat claims.

Algorithm 2 scales relief to verified headcount: 1 to 5 members receive 1 base pack, 6 to 10 members receive 2 base packs, 11 and above receive 3 base packs, plus 2.0 additional kg of rice and 3 additional canned goods per extra member.

Every allocation is based on verified household headcount stored in our database, not on a volunteer's estimate. The scaling is automatic, transparent, and documented for COA verification."
